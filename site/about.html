About ASEAN MAP

The ASEAN Motorcycle Assessment Programme is a regional safety rating for motorcycles. It helps riders choose safer bikes and gives manufacturers a clear reason to build them.

What is ASEAN MAP?

ASEAN MAP is a motorcycle safety rating programme for Southeast Asia, led by Malaysia’s Ministry of Transport through the Malaysian Institute of Road Safety Research (MIROS). It builds on the Malaysia Motorcycle Assessment Programme (MyMAP) and aims to set one harmonised way of judging motorcycle safety across the region.

Each motorcycle model is assessed on four areas and awarded a star rating out of 5. The ratings are published here so that anyone buying a motorcycle can compare models side by side before they decide.

Motorcycles are how millions of people in ASEAN get to work, school and market, and riders make up a large share of road deaths in the region. ASEAN MAP exists to move that number in the right direction by making safety visible, comparable and worth competing on.

The programme in four points

Built on proven work

MyMAP was created by MIROS with the Malaysia Automotive, Robotics and IoT Institute (MARii), drawing on the experience of developing ASEAN NCAP for cars.

Four areas, one rating

UN regulations, conformity of production, safety technology and a support programme are each assessed and combined into a single star rating.

Made for ASEAN roads

The programme is designed around the motorcycles, riders and traffic of Southeast Asia, not copied from a car or European model.

Growing with the region

Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam have committed to take part, with other ASEAN member countries expected to follow.

Why motorcycles needed their own rating

New car assessment programmes have been trusted for decades to push vehicle safety forward. They have helped make most new cars sold in ASEAN markets reasonably safe, but they focus almost entirely on passenger cars. The people most at risk on the region’s roads are riding motorcycles.

A crash-test approach does not translate well to a motorcycle. A rider is not enclosed or strapped in, so the frame cannot protect them the way a car cabin does. A meaningful motorcycle rating has to put more weight on preventing the crash in the first place, with features such as anti-lock brakes, and on whether the bike is built and supported to a consistent standard.

Until recently there was no systematic, science-based way to rate motorcycles, only owner satisfaction surveys and tests of equipment like helmets and jackets. MIROS and MARii set out to fill that gap with MyMAP, which launched in 2021. ASEAN MAP carries the same approach to the wider region, working with partner countries and supported by a project backed by the UN Road Safety Fund.

Our purpose

Safety information that is easy to find, easy to compare and hard to ignore.

Inform riders

Give buyers clear, independent ratings so safety can sit next to price and performance when they choose a motorcycle.

Encourage manufacturers

Reward makers that build in better braking and safety technology, and that stand behind their riders with support programmes.

Support regulators

Align the assessment with UN regulations and give governments a shared reference as they raise motorcycle safety standards.

What we assess

Every motorcycle is scored on four areas, then given a star rating out of 5.

Read the full approach

UN Regulations

Checks that the model meets the relevant UN vehicle regulations, supported by the technical and regulatory documents the manufacturer provides.

Conformity of Production

Looks at the factory to confirm that the motorcycles sold to riders are built consistently to the specification that was assessed.

Safety Technology

Rewards safety features that help prevent a crash, such as anti-lock braking (ABS). Models without key features can lose stars.

Support Programme

Recognises what the manufacturer does beyond the product itself to support rider safety, such as rider awareness and support activities.

Roadmap

ASEAN MAP is being rolled out in phases so that the assessment can grow in depth as the region is ready.

  1. 2021

    MyMAP begins in Malaysia

    MIROS and MARii launch the Malaysia Motorcycle Assessment Programme, the starting point for the regional programme.

  2. 2027 – 2028

    Phase one: documents, features and processes

    Assessment of technical and regulatory documentation, safety features, manufacturing and conformity of production, and manufacturer support programmes.

  3. 2029 – 2034

    Phase two: technical performance

    Gradual introduction of performance assessments such as braking and motorcycle visibility, as the programme keeps pace with new rider-assistance and connected safety technology.

Why ASEAN MAP matters

  • The risk sits with riders. In much of the region, motorcyclists account for a large share of road fatalities, yet passenger cars have had most of the safety attention.
  • Buyers lack comparable information. Without a rating, it is hard to tell which models are better equipped to prevent a crash.
  • One regional standard is stronger than many. A shared framework lets manufacturers, regulators and riders talk about motorcycle safety in the same terms across borders.
