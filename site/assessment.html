Our assessment approach

How ASEAN MAP turns four areas of motorcycle safety into a single star rating out of 5.

Why motorcycles are assessed differently

Car safety ratings rely heavily on crash tests, because a car’s structure and passive safety systems protect the people inside it. Motorcycle makers have fewer options to build that kind of passive protection into a bike, and a rider is not enclosed in the vehicle.

For that reason ASEAN MAP, which builds on Malaysia’s MyMAP program, puts its weight on preventing the crash: active safety features, and the consistent, well-supported production of the motorcycle itself. The assessment is built around four pillars, and the points earned across them decide the star rating.

The program is modelled on successful rating schemes such as ASEAN NCAP for cars, SHARP for helmets and MotoCAP for motorcycle gear.

The four pillars

Every motorcycle model is assessed on each of these areas.

UN Regulations

Does the motorcycle meet recognised international standards?

  • Checks the motorcycle’s compliance with UN regulations on motorcycle safety.
  • Looks at the technical and regulatory documentation the manufacturer provides.
  • Supports ASEAN MAP’s goal of aligning motorcycle safety regulations with UN vehicle safety instruments.

Conformity of Production

Is every motorcycle built to the standard that was assessed?

  • Audits and analyses how the manufacturer builds the model.
  • Covers the manufacturing and production processes behind the motorcycle.
  • In the MyMAP program, these audits take place at the manufacturer’s plant.

Safety Technology

What safety features and new technology does the motorcycle have?

  • Assesses the safety features fitted to the motorcycle, including newer safety technology.
  • Anti-lock braking (ABS) is a key feature: many motorcycles sold in the region lack it, and a model without it can lose stars.
  • In later phases, the program plans to add performance assessments such as braking and visibility.

Support program

What does the manufacturer do to help riders ride safely?

  • Assesses the support program the manufacturer provides to buyers.
  • This includes rider training and education. For example, one manufacturer rated under MyMAP provides safe-riding videos and brochures to its customers.

From points to stars

Each pillar carries its own weightage in the total points. A motorcycle’s star rating is awarded from the points it accumulates across all four pillars, using the same 5-star grading system that car NCAP programs use.

Every rating page in the motorcycle directory shows the model’s score in each pillar and its total score, so you can see where a motorcycle did well and where it lost points, not just its star count.

A reference point: MyMAP

In MyMAP, the program ASEAN MAP builds on, ratings are scored out of 5.00 points. For example, in December 2022 the Modenas Ninja 250 ABS and Z250 ABS each scored 4.78 of 5.00 points and received 5 stars. In that assessment the UN Regulations pillar was worth up to 0.5 points and Conformity of Production up to 1.5 points.

What is assessed, and when

ASEAN MAP is introduced in phases, with more technical testing added over time.

2027 – 2028

Phase one

  • Technical and regulatory documentation
  • Safety features
  • Manufacturing and conformity-of-production processes
  • Support programs provided by manufacturers
2029 – 2034

Phase two

  • Technical performance assessments are progressively added
  • These include braking performance and motorcycle visibility tests
  • MIROS has said the program must stay responsive to new braking, rider-assistance and connected safety technology

How the protocols are being developed

  • Built with partner countries. Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand and Vietnam are the initial partners in developing the assessment protocols.
  • Tested on real motorcycles. Pilot testing is carried out on popular motorcycle models.
  • Shared across sectors. Regional and national working groups bring together government agencies, industry, researchers and civil society.
  • Backed by the UN Road Safety Fund. A project supported by the fund runs from November 2025 to November 2028 to help establish the program.
